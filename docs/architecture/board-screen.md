# Board screen (app)

> How the app draws the board and plays a turn. Code: `apps/mobile/src/features/board/`. Last review: 2026-10-27.

## Parts

| File | What it does |

| `flights.ts` | A move, a hit or an undo as short flights of checkers; a combined move as one flight through its middle points (`pathFlights`). Pure. |
| `skia/MovingCheckers.tsx` | The checker in flight and the checker under the finger (UI thread). |
| `InteractiveBoard.tsx` | The playing board: the pan and tap gestures, selection, highlights and flights. With a new `spaceKey` (the phone turned, #185) it reports no geometry until it has measured its new space. |

The real route is `app/game/[id]/index.tsx`; match end is `app/game/[id]/match-end.tsx` (see [App shell](app-shell.md)). Dev route (release builds redirect it to Home):

- `app/dev/board.tsx`: the fixed positions (`positions.ts`: Portes start, mid-game, bear-off, Plakoto start, Plakoto pins, Fevga start, Asodio start, Asodio loading, Asodio frozen army), from White's and from Black's side. Each position has its direction (`SAMPLE_DIRECTIONS`). The Asodio positions show the frozen marks. The dice switch turns on the dice throw spike (`features/dice-throw`, #183, see [Dice throw spike](../design/dice-throw-spike.md)).

**Game flow and ADR-0003.** The engine's turn flow (#25) is already a pure reducer with every phase in it, so the app wraps it in a small Zustand store and adds no second state machine. ADR-0003 records this (updated in #29).

## Board themes

- Three boards ship: **Walnut Kafeneio** (default), **Olive Inlay** and **Kafeneio Night** (dark, #144) (`packages/themes`). All have the same geometry, so only the art changes.
- The player picks one in **Settings → Board** (`features/settings/BoardThemePicker.tsx`): one radio row per bundled theme, with a small `StaticBoard` of the start position in that theme, and the theme's name and description in the app language. The choice saves at once as `boardTheme` (an unknown id falls back to the default).
- `boardTheme` is separate from `theme` (the app colors, `@tavli/ui`, Settings → Look).
- `useBoardTheme()` reads `boardTheme`. The game screen, the tutorial, the rules pictures (`StartPicture`) and every `StaticBoard` use it. The game sounds come from the board theme too.
- A new theme needs its images in `themeImages.ts` and its sounds in `features/feedback/themeSounds.ts`; tests check both maps. The picker lists it by itself.

## Drawing

- The game screen uses the theme's **portrait-rotated** layout (wireframe option B, "tall", the default) or its **landscape** layout ("classic"), from Settings → Board → Board layout (see [Board layouts](#board-layouts-184)). The tutorial, the rules pictures and the Settings pictures always use the tall one. `fitBoardWithShadow` keeps the aspect ratio and leaves room for the shadow, so the board fits an iPhone SE and large phones.
- Draw order follows `packages/themes/README.md`: shadow, image layers under the points, 24 vector points (color, wood tile, seam), image layers over the points, pinned checkers (Plakoto, Asodio), checkers (frozen ones shaded, Asodio) and count badges, trays, highlights, lamp overlay.
- Until the images load, the board draws with the theme colors, so the first frame is never empty.
- A stack shows at most `checker.maxVisible` (5) checkers. The last one carries a count badge.
- **Bar checkers shrink to fit the bar.** In Walnut Kafeneio the bar is 34 pt and a checker 40 pt, so bar checkers get a radius of 94% of half the bar height.
- Checker colors stay with the player: White uses the theme's `light` style, Black the `dark` style.

## Board layouts (#184)

The owner picked both portrait layouts from the [proposals](../design/game-layout-proposals.md). **Settings → Board → Board layout** (`boardLayout`, `features/settings/BoardLayoutPicker.tsx`) picks one: **Tall** (default; saves from before #184 load as Tall) or **Classic**. Each row has a small picture of the game screen. A change applies at once, also in a running game. `settings_changed` sends the key `boardLayout`.

| | Tall (default) | Classic |
|---|---|---|
| Theme layout | `portrait-rotated`: the board on its side | `landscape`: the board as on a table, seen from your seat |
| Your home (`homeBoardSide` right) | bottom left of the screen | bottom right, as on a real board |
| Board | fills the space between the strips (`flex: 1`) | as wide as the screen: its box has the shape `boxRatioWithShadow` gives (`geometry.ts`), so the board and its shadow fill the width |
| Strips | the menu button (a 36 pt circle with a 44 pt touch area) shares the row with the top strip; your strip under the board | right above and below the board; the board and its strips are centred in the space between the top row and the buttons (`game-middle`) |
| Game label ("Game 2 · Plakoto") | in the menu (`menu-game-label`) | at the top, next to the menu button (and in the menu) |
| Cards (opening, pass, mother) | over the board area | over the whole middle (the board is only about 220 pt tall on an iPhone SE) |
| Notes ("All dice used", loading lock, "Bot plays on.") | at the bottom of the board | at the bottom of the middle, near the buttons |

Both layouts:

- **One place picks the layout.** `boardLayout(theme, mode, side)` in `BoardCanvas.tsx` gives the theme layout, mirrored for the bottom left. `InteractiveBoard` (prop `layout`) and `StaticBoard` fit it. Drawing, hit tests, highlights, ghost targets, combined targets (#180), flights, the Asodio "to enter" stacks and the dice all read that layout, so they all follow. The flip view (pass-and-play) changes only the viewer, as before.
- **The classic art is mirrored for the bottom left.** The landscape art has its trays on one side, so it is not left–right symmetric (`isMirrorSymmetric` is false). `mirrorLayout` then sets `artMirrored`, and `BoardScene` draws the image layers mirrored with the slots (a `Group` with `scaleX: -1` around the board's middle). The board shadow and the lamp stay. The tall art is symmetric and is never mirrored.
- **One-line strips** (36 pt). With a large font or a long Greek name they wrap to two lines; the board then gets a little smaller.
- **Dice on the board.** The dice lie in the middle of the theme's dice area (`boardDiceBox`, `boardDice.ts`): on the tall board in the lower half next to your home, on the classic board in the middle of the right half. A die is the theme's size or at least 32 pt (`MIN_BOARD_DIE`), so it is easy to read (the theme's die is about 25 pt on the tall board of an iPhone SE and 19 pt on the classic one). A double's four dice get 1.25 times the room, so each stays near 25 pt. The dice box takes no touches (`pointerEvents="none"`), so a checker under a die can still be touched. The bot's dice lie there too.
- **The dice canvas keeps its native parent (#173).** `DiceRow` keeps its canvas in its own `collapsable={false}` view, inside the dice box (also a native view: `pointerEvents`, `collapsable={false}`), inside `game-board-layer`. A card over the board hides the dice from screen readers with the board; the card says the roll.
- **One row of two big buttons** at the bottom, in thumb reach: Undo and Confirm share the width (`grow`), or one Roll button, or the bot's box ("Bot rolled 4-2 and is moving…").
- Every text reads upright: nothing on the screen is turned; only the board art lies a quarter turn in Tall.

Sizes (board, checker): Tall on an iPhone SE about 282 × 471 pt, checker 30 pt (before #184: 242 × 404 pt, 26 pt); on a Pro Max 412 × 688 pt, checker 44 pt (before: 372 × 621 pt, 39 pt). Classic on an iPhone SE about 363 × 217 pt, checker 23 pt; on a Pro Max 426 × 255 pt, checker 27 pt. Landscape: see [Landscape](#landscape-185). Not built: anything in the classic layout's free space (proposal question 5), face-to-face.

## Landscape (#185)

The owner's decision: only the **game screen** and the **tutorial board** turn with the phone. Home, New game, Settings, History, Rules, match end and the welcome screens stay upright. In landscape the whole game UI turns, so every text reads upright.

**Orientation.** `app.json` allows every orientation (`"orientation": "default"`), so a screen can turn. The root `Stack` locks every screen upright (`orientation: 'portrait_up'` in `screenOptions`, `src/app/_layout.tsx`); the game route and the tutorial route follow the phone (`orientation: 'default'`: portrait and both landscapes, never upside down on an iPhone). This is react-native-screens' per-screen orientation, the way the Expo docs recommend with Expo Router; no extra native module. The phone's own rotation lock still wins. The menu and the resign dialogs are a `Modal` (`Sheet`): on iOS a `Modal` turns the screen back to portrait unless it lists landscape, so the game's `Sheet` passes `turns` (`supportedOrientations`). Other sheets stay portrait only.

**Layout choice.** `useScreenLayout` (`screenLayout.ts`) reads the window (`useWindowDimensions`): wider than tall is `landscape`, else the Board layout setting (`tall` or `classic`). Landscape always draws the classic board (`boardModeOf`), whatever the setting; turning back to portrait brings the setting back.

| Part | Landscape |
|---|---|
| Board | the classic board (`landscape` theme layout) in the middle, as large as the space between the columns allows (`flex: 1`, `fitBoardWithShadow`) |
| Left column (`game-left`) | the menu button, the other player's strip, your strip, and Undo under the left thumb. The strips are `stacked`: the color and the score, the turn pill, the name, the pips. The other player's strip sits at the top (next to their side of the board), yours at the bottom. |
| Right column (`game-right`) | the game label at the top, an empty slot for the dice, Roll (or Confirm, or the bot's box) under the right thumb. The column lies over the right part of the board layer (`position: absolute`). The top part (`game-right-top`) is exactly as tall as the space above the dice, the slot (`game-dice-slot`) as tall as the dice box (four dice for a double), and the bottom part (`game-right-bottom`) takes the rest. The top and bottom parts scroll on their own, so a large font never puts text over the dice. The slot stays before a roll too, so Roll and Confirm sit in the same place. |
| Column width | `SIDE_COLUMN` (128 pt), up to 1.35 times that with a large font (`sideColumnWidth`). The longest label is Greek "Επιβεβαίωση" (about 100 pt at 17 pt); the side buttons are `compact` (4 pt side padding, no minimum width), so it fits on one line. |
| Dice | in the right column's slot, as large as the column allows (32 to 40 pt, `columnDiceBox`). The board layer reaches under the right column (`paddingRight` of the column width; the board keeps clear of it), so the dice box lies inside the layer, never outside its parent. So the dice canvas never changes its native parent when the phone turns (#173), and a roll does not start again. The cards, the notes and the move list keep clear of the column (`besideColumn`). |
| Safe areas | the screen keeps `insets.left`, `insets.right` and `insets.bottom` free (the notch or Dynamic Island, the round corners, the home bar). |
| Cards (opening, pass, mother) | over the board, between the columns; they scroll when they are taller than the screen |
| Game-end sheet, menu, dialogs | at most `MAX_SHEET_WIDTH` (560 pt) wide, in the middle; they scroll |
| Move list | under the board |
| Large fonts | both columns are scroll views, so nothing is cut; the columns grow a little and the board gets smaller |

Sizes (board, checker, point): iPhone SE 667 × 375 pt: 389 × 233 pt, checker 25 pt, point 27 pt (the width limits it; about 130 pt stay free above and below). Pro Max 956 × 440 pt (62 pt safe areas left and right, 21 pt at the bottom): 556 × 333 pt, checker 35 pt, point 38 pt. The proposal estimated 28 and 37 pt with 96 pt columns; those columns are too narrow for Greek.

**Turning mid-turn keeps everything.** Every layout keeps the board at the same place in the React tree: `game-content` → `game-middle` → the board frame → `game-board-layer`. Only the views around it change (the top row or the left column before it, the strips and the buttons or the right column after it). So the board (with a picked-up checker), the dice canvas and an open card stay mounted, and the views around the board never flatten (`collapsable={false}`), so the canvases keep their native parent. The game state lives in the store anyway. Checkers in the air just land when the board's space changes (their flight belongs to the old board place). The tutorial does the same: the lesson column on the left in landscape, the board in the same place in the tree.

**The dice wait for the new board place.** `useScreenLayout` also gives a `spaceKey` (the layout, the window size and the font scale). When it changes (the phone turns, a split screen resizes, the font size changes), the board is still drawn in its old space for a moment. `InteractiveBoard` then keeps drawing the old layout at the old size, measures its new space (its layout event, or `measure` right after the change when the size stays the same) and only then reports the new geometry. `GameScreen` keeps the geometry with the key it was measured for. Until the new one comes, the dice stay mounted (no new roll) but hide (`opacity: 0`), so they never sit in a wrong place or jump.

**Tutorial in landscape.** The lesson (title, Skip, the lesson text, the move list, the dice and Next) sits in a left column (38% of the width, 240–340 pt) that scrolls as a whole; the classic board fills the rest. Next sits under the dice, as wide as the column, so Greek or a large font has room. In portrait it keeps the tall board, with the dice and Next side by side.

## Plakoto, Fevga and Asodio

The board reads the game from the engine state: `rules.movement.direction` gives the direction (`turnView`, `GameScreen`), and the legal moves come from the engine (`legalNextMoves`) as in Portes. So drag, tap, hints, undo and confirm work the same in every game. Plakoto and Fevga never use the bar; only Portes hits. Asodio uses the bar halves for its checkers that are not entered yet (see [Asodio](#asodio)).

- **Plakoto pins.** A pinned checker lies under the checkers that pin it. It sits at the base of the point, where a lone checker would be; the stack on top starts `PIN_PEEK` (0.6) checker radii further along the point, so the pinned checker peeks out on the base side (wireframe 17's small picture). It also gets a ring in the theme's badge color (`PIN_RING_WIDTH`), so a pin never looks like a normal stack. The pinned checker is not part of the stack: `countIn` does not count it, `pinnedIn` says it is there, and `layoutBoard` returns it in `pins` (key `<slot>:pinned`). Touch, ghost targets and flights use the same pinned stack positions (`stackGeometry(…, pinned)`), so a pin lands exactly where it is drawn. A pin is a plain move: no checker flies to the bar.
- **Screen readers:** the board has a label, "Board", plus one line per pinned checker ("Bob's checker on point 24 is pinned", in the owner's own numbers). Asodio adds its own lines (below).
- **Fevga** (`direction: "parallel"`): both players start on their own point 24, in diagonally opposite corners (for White at the bottom: White on layout 24, Black on layout 12), and both move the same way round. This is only the point mapping in `boardModel.ts` (`absoluteToOwn`); the drawing and hit-testing rects are the same as in Portes. Tests check it in the portrait and the landscape layout.
- The `plakoto` and `fevga` presets keep the Tavli rotation (Portes → Plakoto → Fevga), so a match of the `plakoto` preset starts with Portes. For a match of one game only, override the rotation: `resolveRules('plakoto', { match: { rotation: ['plakoto'] } })` (the tests do this).
- The trays stay where the theme puts them. In Fevga the top player's home is the top right, but its tray stays at the bottom right.

### Asodio

The owner version (`asodio.kafeneio`, `docs/rules/asodio.md` A1–A6). The engine decides every move; `features/board/asodio.ts` only reads the board and the rules, so the screen can say why a checker does not move.

- **The "to enter" stacks.** A checker that is not entered yet (`board.outside`) waits on its player's half of the bar. In the portrait layout each half sits right above that player's starting quarter (spot 6, own point 19), so a checker enters a short hop away. The stack always shows its count badge, even when every checker is drawn (`StackModel.toEnter`). The owner version pins and never hits, so nothing else uses the bar. The strip under the name says "12 to enter".
- **Entering.** Tap or drag from the stack to spot `d` (own point `25 - d`), like any other move: the engine's moves come `from: 'outside'`, and `slotOfOrigin('outside')` is the bar half. A double enters four on one spot, one tap pair each (A1). Undo flies the checker back to the stack. While loading, only the stack has moves (A2), because the engine lists only entries.
- **Loading lock.** While all 15 are still outside (`firstEntryGate`), a note over the board says "Roll 1-2 or a double to start" before the roll (`loading-lock-hint`; "Roll a double to start" with `firstEntryGateRolls: "doubleOnly"`). A roll that does not open the board is a pass; the pass card says "Board locked" and what opens it. An opening roll is never a double, so most games start with a pass (the pass card then replaces the opening result).
- **Frozen checkers.** The quarter lock freezes every checker in front of the player's rear quarter (`frozenPoints`). They get a soft dark shade and a thin frost ring (`FROZEN_MARK` in `BoardScene.tsx`), quiet on purpose: "waits", not "danger". Both players' frozen checkers are marked, all the time (`asodioMarks` → `layoutBoard(…, marks)` → `PlacedChecker.frozen`). Nothing is marked while loading: every checker is still in the starting quarter.
- **Frozen army (A6).** When every checker in the rear quarter is pinned (`armyFrozen`), the player passes until it is free. The strip says "Army frozen", the pinned checker shows the Plakoto pin ring, the rest are shaded, and the pass card is titled "Army frozen" and says the last checker is pinned.
- **Pins** use the Plakoto drawing (`PIN_PEEK`, `PIN_RING_WIDTH`).
- **Time-out** (#170, `reason: 'timeout'`): an online turn timer ran out. The engine scores it like a resign of the game (1 point, or 2 when the loser bore off no checker), and the chip follows those points. The reason line names the player: "Maria ran out of time." The sheet reads the line with the rest of the result (`useAnnouncement` in `GameEndSheet`); `turnAnnouncement` says nothing once the game is over, so nothing is read twice. Online play is not wired to the app yet: tests send the `timeout` action to the store (`GameScreen.timeout.test.tsx`).
- **Draw** (A5, `reason: 'deadlock'`): the game-end sheet says "The game is a draw", the chip "Draw · 0 points" and the reason "No checker can ever move again." The next game starts with a new opening roll. A Plakoto draw (both mothers pinned) uses the same chip with its own reason.
- **Screen readers:** the board label adds "Ann has 12 checkers to enter" (or "You have …"), "Bob's checkers on points 18, 16 are frozen" and "Ann's last checker is pinned: the whole army is frozen". The strips read their notes too.
- **Next player:** the pass card names the player to move from the engine (`state.toMove`), never "the other one", because some Asodio presets give the same player another roll.
- The app plays only the owner version. Other presets (hits, a chosen double after 1-2, extra rolls) work in the engine and on the board screen (below).
- **A chosen double** (Acey-Deucey presets, #202): the answer sets the dice and the turn starts with no roll (`diceWereRolled` is false; the dice show at rest, see Doubles below). Screen readers hear "You chose double 4: four moves of 4." («Διάλεξες τεσσάρες: τέσσερις κινήσεις του 4.», the same Greek names as a roll), or "Bob played: …. Bob chose double 4: four moves of 4.": the 1-2 turn ends on the question, so it has no line of its own.

### Choose a double (Acey-Deucey Asodio)

In the Acey-Deucey presets a played 1-2 makes the engine flow stop in `awaiting-answer`, and `currentPrompt` asks the same player `chooseDouble` (choices `1` to `6`). The screen picks the card by `prompt.kind`: `chooseDouble` gets this card, any other question the mother card.

- **A person answers** (#205): a card over the board, "You rolled 1-2" (pass-and-play: "Ann, you rolled 1-2"), "Choose a double:", and six buttons (`DoubleChoices.tsx`). Each button shows the two dice of the double (plain views in the theme's dice colors, no canvas) and its name: "double 4", in Greek «ασσάκια», «δυάρες», «τριάρες», «τεσσάρες», «πεντάρες», «εξάρες» (`game.doubles.name.N`). Greek: «Έφερες ασόδυο (1-2)», «Διάλεξε ποιες διπλές θα παίξεις:». It cannot be closed without a choice. The answer is the engine action `answer`; the turn then starts with the chosen dice at rest (see Doubles below).
- **Layout:** three buttons a row (two rows), two a row from font scale 1.5 (`WIDE_FONT_SCALE`), so the names never break. The card sits in the card overlay like the others: it covers the board on the Tall and the Classic board, and in landscape it stays beside the right column. It scrolls when a large font makes it taller than its space.
- **Screen readers:** the card hides the board, takes focus and reads itself ("You rolled 1-2 Choose a double:"), like every card (#153). Each button reads "double 4: four moves of 4" («τεσσάρες: τέσσερις κινήσεις του 4»); the dice pictures are hidden. The move list hides while the card asks, so the card's buttons are the choices; after the answer the list offers the moves of the chosen double.
- **The bot answers** by itself in its turn (`botStep` → `'answer'`), so no card shows. Its box then says "Bot chose 4-4 and is moving…", not "rolled".
- **No move with the chosen double:** the flow passes at once. The pass card says "Ann chose 6-6. No checker can move." («Διάλεξες 6-6. …»), not "rolled". The state no longer holds the dice, so `diceWereChosen(flow, dice)` (`features/game/rolledDice.ts`) reads the log: the dice were chosen when the last event that gave dice is the answer with their value.

### The mother question (Plakoto)

With `motherRule: "offerConcede"` (the app default) the engine flow stops in `awaiting-answer` after a turn that pins a mother, and `currentPrompt` names the pinned player.

- **A person answers** (pass-and-play, or you vs the bot): a card over the board (wireframe 17), "Bob, your mother is pinned" (or "Your mother is pinned"), what happened and what Resign costs, and two buttons of equal weight: **Resign (lose 2)** and **Play on**. It cannot be closed without a choice. In pass-and-play the pinned player answers; with Flip on, the board turns to them first. The answer is the engine action `answer`.
- **The bot answers** its own question in its turn (`botStep` → `'answer'` in `features/bot/botTurn.ts`). It asks the runner (`BotRunner.chooseAnswer`, #106; easy and medium play on). A runner without `chooseAnswer` (the method is optional), or an answer the question does not offer, falls back to "play on" (`botAnswer`); if the engine still refuses, the bot turn reports an error instead of waiting forever. A note over the board says "Bot plays on." for 2 s (`BOT_ANSWER_NOTE_MS`). If the bot resigns, the game-end sheet says so.

## Dice

- The UI never picks dice. Every roll is an engine action (`roll`, `rollOpening`); the flow rolls with the `Rng` from `services.ts` (`createLocalRng`).
- The animation (`diceMotion.ts`) is a 2D tumble on the UI thread: the die spins, hops and grows, and its face flips through the other numbers in a fixed order. From 78% of the roll on, it shows the engine's value.
- **Opening roll (#176):** a card with one die per player and the names under the dice. The owner's play test found "?" boxes instead of dice, no clear "tap to roll", and a tie that went by too fast to see. Now:
  - Before a roll each die is a blank die face (the theme's die body, no pips). Each die has one Skia canvas for the life of the card, in its own `collapsable={false}` view (#173).
  - The big **Roll** button sits on the card, under the dice, with the player's name in pass-and-play ("Maria: roll", "or tap the board"). The controls row is empty during the opening. In pass-and-play each player taps for their own die (`openingTaps="perPlayer"`), and the card says "Maria rolled 5." after the first die. Vs the bot one tap rolls both dice (`"one"`): the bot's die rolls with yours, by itself ("Roll to start").
  - Each die tumbles and lands as in the controls row. Each die is keyed on the round (`OpeningStatus.round`, from `opening.ts`), so a die rolls again only in a new round.
  - **Tie:** the engine clears the round at once, but `openingStatus` returns `kind: 'tie'` until the next roll. The card keeps both dice on the tied value and says "Tie: both rolled 4. Roll again." It never moves on by itself. For `OPENING_TIE_WAIT_MS` (1 s) after a tie the Roll button is off and a board tap does nothing, so the dice land first and a quick second tap does not skip the tie.
  - **Result:** "Maria starts with 6-3." (vs bot "You start with 6-3."), the starter's name in the turn color, an **OK** button and "Closes by itself after 3 s." It stays until OK, a tap on the board, or `OPENING_RESULT_MS` (3 s). The starter then plays both opening dice.
  - Screen readers: focus moves to the title when the card opens (#153). Each step is read (`useAnnouncement`, keyed on the step and the round, so a second tie of 4 is read again): "Each player rolls one die. … Maria: roll.", "Maria rolled 5. Nikos: roll.", "Tie: both rolled 4. Roll again. Maria: roll.", "Maria starts with 6-3." Each die is an image: "Not rolled yet" or "Rolled 5".
- A haptic and a sound play when the last die lands (`feedback.event('diceLand')`).
- **Doubles (#177).** The owner's play test: "the 4 dice show up before the user can understand he rolled doubles." Now a double shows the two rolled dice first, like any roll:
  - The two dice roll and land (`DICE_LAND_MS`, 740 ms). They stay for a beat (`DOUBLE_BEAT_MS`, 600 ms). Then they split into four (`DOUBLE_SPLIT_MS`, 300 ms): each rolled die slides to the top row of the 2 x 2 grid and shrinks, and a copy slides out from under it to the bottom row. The four dice are in place `DOUBLE_SHOWN_MS` (1.64 s) after the roll. The times are in `diceMotion.ts`.
  - At the split a short **"Doubles!"** label shows over the bottom of the board for `DOUBLE_LABEL_MS` (1.5 s). It is a status note like "All dice used"; that note and "Bot plays on." win when they show. It is a note over the board, so the board and the buttons do not change size. Screen readers skip the label (`accessible={false}`): the roll announcement already says it.
  - Greek names each double by its value, not with a word for "doubles" (the owner's words): 1-1 «ασσάκια», 2-2 «δυάρες», 3-3 «τριάρες», 4-4 «τεσσάρες» (not «ντόρτια»), 5-5 «πεντάρες», 6-6 «εξάρες». So the label is «Εξάρες!» for 6-6, and screen readers hear «Έφερες εξάρες: τέσσερις κινήσεις του 6.». Each value has its own keys (`game.doubles.name.N` for the sentence, `game.doubles.label.N` for the label). English says "Doubles!" and "You rolled double 6: four moves of 6.".
  - `useDoubleReveal` (in `GameScreen`) runs the timers and passes `split` to `DiceRow`. A move before the split shows the four dice at once (a faded die must be one of four), with no "Doubles!" label later, and they stay four after an undo. Dice at rest (the pass card, the tutorial) show four at once. So does a double that no roll gave: in the Acey-Deucey presets of Asodio a 1-2 lets the player choose a double (`answer`). `diceWereRolled` (`features/game/rolledDice.ts`) checks the flow log: the dice were rolled when the last roll of the game has the dice the state holds. Chosen dice get `rollKey` `null`: no tumble, no pair, no split, no label. The bot moves a chosen double after the think time only (no `afterRollMs`, no `doubleMs`), as for any dice it did not roll.
  - Used dice still fade one by one, from the first die of the grid on.
  - On the board (Tall and Classic, #184) the dice box is the box of the four dice from the roll on, so it does not move at the split. The two rolled dice keep the size of any roll (`boardDiceBox` gives `pairSize`); only the grid dice get the 1.25 times room.   - All of it happens in the one dice canvas, which stays in its `collapsable={false}` view (#173). The dice of the split are Skia nodes, not views, so nothing moves to another native parent.
  - The bot's doubles split the same way. Its first move waits `doubleMs` more (see the bot timing below), so it starts after the four dice show.

## Bot turn timing (#172)

The owner's play test said the bot moved too fast to follow. Now each step of the bot's turn stays on screen long enough to see. The numbers are in `BOT_SPEED_TIMING` (`features/bot/botSpeed.ts`, a leaf module that Settings and `botTurn.ts` both use). **Settings → Game → Bot speed** picks the row: **Slow**, **Normal** (default) or **Fast**. A new speed applies from the bot's next turn.

| Step | Slow | Normal | Fast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wait before the roll (`beforeRollMs`) | 1.3 s | 0.9 s | 0.6 s |
| Least time from the bot's own roll to its first move (`afterRollMs`) | 1.92 s | 1.52 s | 1.17 s |
| Extra time after a double, which splits into four dice first (`doubleMs`, #177) | 0.9 s | 0.9 s | 0.9 s |
| Time from one move to the next (`moveMs`) | 1.3 s | 0.9 s | 0.55 s |
| Extra pause after a hit, before the next move (`hitMs`) | 0.7 s | 0.5 s | 0.3 s |
| Time from the last move to the confirm (`landMs`) | 0.6 s | 0.45 s | 0.35 s |

- **The dice stay on screen.** The bot thinks (`thinkingDelayMs` from `@tavli/bot`) while the dice roll. Right after its own roll, its first move waits for the longer of the think time and `afterRollMs`. `afterRollMs` is the time the two dice land (`DICE_LAND_MS`: `ROLL_MS` + `ROLL_STAGGER_MS`, 740 ms) plus a pause. After a double it waits `doubleMs` more: the beat and the split (`DOUBLE_BEAT_MS` + `DOUBLE_SPLIT_MS`, 900 ms). So the four dice get the same pause as any other roll before the first checker moves. `doubleMs` is `DOUBLE_MS` at every speed, because the split does not depend on the bot speed; `botTurn.test.ts` checks that it equals `DOUBLE_BEAT_MS` + `DOUBLE_SPLIT_MS`.
- **Dice it did not just roll** get only the think time: the opening dice (they were on the opening card), and a turn that goes on after the menu or Settings closes.
- **Each move lands first.** A checker flies for `FLIGHT_MS` (200 ms); a hit checker leaves `HIT_DELAY_MS` (120 ms) later. `moveMs` is at least that plus 200 ms at every speed, so the next move starts after the last one has landed. `botTurn.test.ts` checks this.
- **A hit gets a short extra pause** (`hitMs`), so the player sees the checker go to the bar. A pin (Plakoto) is a plain move.
- **After the last move** the bot waits only until the checker lands, plus a short beat: `landMs` (the 200 ms flight and 150–400 ms), and `HIT_DELAY_MS` more after a hit so the hit checker lands too. Then it confirms. The full `moveMs` gap is only between moves.
- Tests move the fake clock by these constants, never by fixed numbers. Screen tests pass their own short `timing` (`MatchScreen` `botTiming`, `GameScreen` `bot.timing`).

## Sound and haptics

- One call per game event: `feedback.event('checkerPlace')`. `features/feedback/feedback.ts` (`createGameFeedback`) looks up the theme's `sounds[event]` and `haptics[event]` and plays both. It drops an event that comes too soon after the same one (`MIN_GAP_MS`, e.g. 70 ms for checker clicks).
- Two ports: `SoundPlayer` (adapter `@tavli/audio-expo`, expo-audio) and `Feedback` (haptics, adapter `@tavli/haptics-expo`). `appSound` follows Settings → Sound, `appFeedback` follows Settings → Haptics.
- `features/game/gameFeedback.ts` (`useGameFeedback`, used by `GameScreen`) reads the engine's event log after each store change: a roll plays `diceRoll`; a move plays `checkerPlace`, `checkerHit` (hit or pin) or `bearOff`; the next player's turn plays `turnStart`; the end plays `gameWin` or `matchWin`. An action the engine refuses plays `invalidMove`. So drag, tap, bot and replay all sound the same, and screens need no extra calls.
- UI-only events (`diceLand`, `checkerPickUp`) use the `event` function that `useGameFeedback` returns.
- The theme's sounds are preloaded when the game screen opens. Sounds mix with other apps' audio and follow the phone's silent switch.

## Moving checkers

- **Touch** a checker that can move: it gets a ring, and each legal target gets a dashed ring and a ghost checker where it would land (theme `highlights`). Targets come from the engine (`legalNextMoves`); the app never decides what is legal.
- **Two gestures race** (#174; Gesture Handler 3, `useCompetingGestures(pan, tap)`, settings in `BOARD_GESTURES` in `interaction.ts`). The first to activate cancels the other.
  - The **pan** (`usePanGesture`, `minDistance: 8`) is the drag. It activates once the finger moves 8 pt (`TAP_SLOP`).
  - The **tap** (`useTapGesture`, `maxDistance: 8`, `maxDuration: 1500`) is tap-tap. It fails once the finger moves more than 8 pt, or stays down longer than 1.5 s (`TAP_MAX_MS`). So a slow press (touch, wait, lift) still selects.
  - Why not one pan: a pan only activates on a move. A finger that lifts first makes the pan FAIL, so its `onFinalize` gets `canceled: true`. The old board used one pan with `minDistance: 0` and read a tap from a normal end. On a phone a quick tap has no move, so it was lost. Only a long press worked, because a held finger shakes a little, which activated the pan.
  - The board is not inside a `ScrollView`. The game and tutorial screens only show a `ScrollView` over the board for a card, and then the card takes the touches on purpose.
- **Drag and drop:** the drag runs on the UI thread: the picked-up checker follows the finger through shared values (`dragPoint`). Only the result (picked up, dropped at x, y) goes to the JS thread. `panEnd` says what the end of the pan means: a drop, a cancel, or nothing (it never dragged).
- **Tap-tap:** tap a checker, then tap a target. A target wins over another movable checker on that point. A tap may miss a target by 22 pt (wireframe, iPhone SE). A swipe that does not start on a movable checker does nothing.
- A drop on the source cancels quietly. A drop anywhere else is illegal: the checker flies back in 160 ms, with the theme's `invalidMove` haptic. A drag the system cancels (a call, the app goes to the background) also flies back, with no move.
- **Hit-testing uses the drawing rects.** Sources and targets use `slotRect` (the same point rect that holds the triangle and the stack), the bar halves and the trays.
- **Flights:** a move flies the checker to its landing place (200 ms); a hit sends the hit checker to the bar a moment later; a pin (Plakoto) only flies the pinner. While a checker flies, the checker at its landing place is hidden. The move that ends a turn (auto-confirm) has no flight when the board turns to the next player.
- Two dice that reach the same target (bearing off): the lower die is used, so the higher one stays.

### One checker, both dice (#180)

The owner's play test: "Say I rolled a 2 and a 3. I should see what a 2 move and then a 3 move with the selected piece looks like, and tap the 2+3 target and go straight there."

- **Targets.** A selected (or dragged) checker lights its single-die targets and its **combined** targets: 2+3 for a normal roll; 2, 3 and 4 steps of a double (4, 6, 8 pips with 2-2). A combined target has a dotted ring with no fill and one small dot per die inside (`COMBINED_MARK` in `skia/BoardScene.tsx`, `Mark.steps`), so it does not rely on color. The single targets are the middle points of the path, so the path is lit too.
- **Only the engine's moves.** `combinedPaths` (`combined.ts`) follows one checker through the engine's lists: the first step from `legalNextMoves`, each next step from `legalNextMoves` after the steps before it, from the point where the checker landed. `flowPaths(game)` plays each step with the engine's own reducer (`applyGameAction`). The app adds no rule check, so "use both dice", "play the larger die", the bar, Plakoto pins, the Fevga rules and the Asodio locks hold by themselves. A target that one die already reaches is not offered again; after a step to "off" the path ends.
- **Which order.** When both orders reach the target, the app plays (1) the shorter path (bearing off), then (2) the order with fewer hits or pins on the way, then (3) the lower die first ("a 2 and then a 3"). So a path that hits (Portes) or pins (Plakoto) on a middle point is offered only when it is the only legal order; then it plays the hit or pin on the way. A player who wants the hit plays the two dice one by one. The checker's last step may hit or pin like any move.
- **Playing it.** A tap or a drop on a combined target sends the steps to the engine one by one (`InteractiveBoard` `onSteps` → `GameScreen` `playSteps`). Auto-confirm goes only with the last step. If the engine refused a step, the steps before it would be undone, so nothing is half played. The checker flies one path (`pathFlights`, `Flight.via`): one 200 ms leg per die, each leg slowing down at its end, so it stops on each middle point. A checker hit on the way leaves when the mover gets there.
- **Taps.** A combined target is a target like any other: it wins over another movable checker on that point (e.g. your own stack on 6 after 13/7/6). Single targets come first in the list, so a tap that reaches both picks the single one.
- **Not in the tutorial.** The tutorial passes no `paths`, so each lesson still plays its moves one die at a time.
- Haptics from the theme: `checkerPickUp`, `checkerPlace`, `checkerHit`, `invalidMove`.
- **Tutorial props** (#128): `cue` rings every movable checker while none is selected ("touch this one"; `BoardHighlights.cues`, drawn like the selected ring). `onMissTap` reports a tap that missed every target after a checker was picked. Games use neither.
- Not built: the optional double-tap shortcut (it would delay every single tap), and the dashed "last move" arrow from the wireframes.

## Turn: undo, confirm, turn indicator

- **Controls row** (wireframes 6b–6f): one big Roll button before the roll; then **Undo · Confirm**, two big buttons side by side (#184). The dice lie on the board.
- **Undo** takes back the last single move (`undo` action); the checker flies back, and a hit checker comes back from the bar. Disabled (dashed) when there is nothing to undo.
- **Undo after a combined move** (#180) takes back the whole move: one engine `undo` per step, and one flight back along the path. `GameScreen` keeps a note of each combined move (`moveGroups.ts`: the turn's start state, the moves played right after it, its size). Undo takes back the group when the last moves of the turn are still that group; a single move played after it comes back first, on its own. The engine still sees single moves, so `undo_used` is sent once per step.
- **Confirm** is enabled only when the engine says `canConfirm` (all dice used, or no move left). Then a hint shows: "All dice used. Confirm or undo."
- **Auto-confirm** (Settings, default off): each move is sent with `autoConfirm`, so the engine ends a complete turn at once.
- **Turn indicator:** the active player's strip gets a thick border and a pill: "YOUR TURN", "PLAYING" for the bot, or the player's name in pass-and-play ("MARIA'S TURN"). Pip counts follow the moves of the turn; Settings → Show pip counts hides them.
- **No legal moves:** the engine passes the turn by itself. The card says who rolled what and that the turn passes; the dice stay, faded. Your own pass (in pass-and-play: the player whose turn it was) waits for OK, so there is time to read why (#178). The bot's pass closes by itself after 3.5 s (`BOT_PASS_NOTICE_MS`, "Closes by itself after 3.5 s."), or sooner on OK; the timer waits while the screen is paused (menu). The bot's turn starts only when no card covers the board, so after your pass it starts when you tap OK. Until the card closes the board stays with the player who passed (flip). Asodio has two more titles: "Board locked" (the loading lock) and "Army frozen" (A6).
- **Game end** (`GameEndSheet`): a sheet over the final board, with a dim layer over the rest. Winner (or "The game is a draw"), Mono / Diplo chip (diplo filled; "Draw · 0 points" for a draw), a reason line ("Bob did not bear off any checker." for a diplo, "Mono: Maria resigned the game." or "Diplo: Maria resigned before bearing off a checker." for a resigned game (the diplo reason only when the loser bore off no checker, #181), "No checker can ever move again." for an Asodio deadlock, "Maria ran out of time." / "You ran out of time." when an online turn timer ran out, #170), the match box ("You 2 – 0 Bot", "First to 3 points"), **Next game** (the winner starts) and **Home** (a text button).
- **Match end:** no sheet for the last game. The final position stays for `MATCH_END_DELAY_MS` (1 s), so the last checker lands, then `GameScreen` calls `onMatchOver` and the route opens the match-end screen ([App shell](app-shell.md)). It waits while the screen is paused (menu or Settings on top).
- **Celebration** (`Celebration.tsx`): only when a person wins (`isHumanWin` in `gameFeedback.ts`, the same rule as the win jingle). Vs the bot, a bot win shows none. Fourteen confetti pieces fly out on one shared Reanimated value, and the icon pops (🎉 on game end, 🏆 on match end). With the system **Reduce motion** setting on (`useReducedMotion`), only the still icon shows. Screen readers skip it.
- **Screen readers:** the game-end sheet and the match-end screen announce the result when they show ("Ann wins the game! Diplo · +2 points … Ann 2 – 0 Bob"). The sheet is modal for VoiceOver (`accessibilityViewIsModal`). Titles are headers; every button has a label and is at least 44 pt tall.
- **Cards hide what they cover (#153).** While a card shows over the board (opening, pass, "Board locked", "Army frozen", mother question, choose a double), screen readers skip the board layer (`game-board-layer`: `importantForAccessibility="no-hide-descendants"` and `accessibilityElementsHidden`), like the tutorial's mother card (#151). The strips, the menu button and the controls stay reachable. The opening Roll button is on the opening card (#176). While the game-end sheet shows, or the menu or a resign dialog is open (`covered`, from `MatchScreen`), they skip the whole screen under it (`game-content`). The menu and dialogs are a `Modal` (`components/Sheet.tsx`). When a card or the game-end sheet opens, focus moves to its title (`useFocusOnShow`, `features/accessibility/focus.ts`, through `AccessibilityInfo.sendAccessibilityEvent`); the card is then announced as before. The cards over the board, the game-end sheet and `Sheet` scroll when a large font makes them taller than the space they have. The status notes over the board (loading lock, "Bot plays on.", "All dice used") sit outside the hidden layer, so screen readers still hear them. A new pass is a new card (`key={pass.key}`): focus moves again and it is read again. The game-end confetti is drawn outside the sheet's scroll view, so nothing clips it.
- **Canvases stay in their native view (#173).** React Native often puts the children of a plain `View` straight into a native view higher up ("view flattening"). The hiding props above make `game-content` and `game-board-layer` hold their children only while they hide. So their children used to move to another native parent each time a card, the menu or the game-end sheet opened or closed. On iOS a Skia canvas that moves loses its drawing, and it stays blank until it draws again: the dice vanished after the menu or Settings and came back after a move. Both views now have `collapsable={false}`, so they always hold their children and nothing under them moves. `DiceRow` and each opening die (`OpeningCard`, #176) also keep their canvas in their own `collapsable={false}` view. A new view that turns such props on and off needs `collapsable={false}` too. `apps/mobile/test/nativeParent.ts` follows React Native's flattening rules; tests use it to check that a canvas keeps its native parent.
- **Check on a device (#153):** (1) the card title may be read twice: once by the focus move and once at the start of the announcement. We keep the full announcement, so nothing is lost if the focus move fails. (2) `Sheet` uses `maxHeight: '100%'` with a top inset margin; with a large font on Android edge-to-edge it may reach under the status bar. (3) Focus is not moved back when the menu, a dialog or the game-end sheet closes; check where VoiceOver and TalkBack land. (4) Focus moves with no delay; on iOS it may need a short one.
- The opening result closes after 3 s (`OPENING_RESULT_MS`), or at once on OK or a tap anywhere on the board. A tie stays until the next roll (#176).
- A rematch opens a new board screen, so the closed cards of the last match are forgotten.

## Accessibility

The Skia board takes no screen reader actions, so a screen reader user plays with buttons (#134). Code: `apps/mobile/src/features/accessibility/`.

- **Move list** (`MoveList.tsx`, `moveList.ts`): a row of buttons under the bottom strip, one per legal next move. The moves are the board's moves (`legal`, from `legalNextMoves`), so the list never decides what is legal. Two dice that reach the same target give one button, with the lower die (the tap-tap rule). The checker furthest back comes first.
- **Combined moves** (#180): the list also has a button for each of the board's combined targets (`listChoices`, the same `paths`). It reads each step: "Checker from point 13 to point 10, then to point 5" (", hits" or ", pins" after the step that does it, "then bear off"); the button shows "13 → 10 → 5". One Undo takes it back whole, as on the board.
- **Labels** use the mover's own point numbers, like the rest of the screen: "Checker from point 13 to point 8, hits", "From the bar to point 22", "Enter on point 22" (Asodio, from `outside`), "Bear off from point 6", ", pins" for a pin. The button shows the short form ("13 → 8 · hit"); the screen reader reads the full one. Strings are in `game.moveList` (en and el).
- A button plays the move like a tap-tap: same `playMove`, same auto-confirm, same flight (none before the board has its size). **Undo** and **Confirm** stay the normal buttons ("↶ Undo" reads "Undo"). The list hides while a card covers the board, during the bot's turn and when no move is left.
- **When it shows** (`screenReader.ts`): Settings → Accessibility → Move list. `auto` (default) shows it while a screen reader is on (`AccessibilityInfo.isScreenReaderEnabled` and the `screenReaderChanged` event); `on` shows it for everyone; `off` never.
- **Tutorial:** the lesson's own moves (`lessonMoves`) feed the list, so it offers only the moves the lesson teaches, and each lesson (Portes, Plakoto and Fevga, #149, Asodio, #159) can be finished with it.
- **Announcements** (`announceForAccessibility`, `features/game/announcements.ts`): after a roll, "Bob rolled 5-3." (or "You rolled 5-3."); after a double, "You rolled double 4: four moves of 4." once, at the roll, not again at the split (#177); after a chosen double (Acey-Deucey, after a 1-2), "You chose double 4: four moves of 4." (#202); after another player's turn, "Bob played: Checker from point 13 to point 8; …. Your turn." Vs the bot only the bot's turns are read; in pass-and-play every turn, for the next player. Each event has a key, so the same text is read again for a new roll. The opening card, the cards over the board (pass, mother question, choose a double) and the game-end sheet read themselves (no more Android-only live regions), so nothing is read twice: the dice line skips the opening dice and a pass (the pass card says the roll).
- **Text size:** text follows the system font. No text has a line limit (`numberOfLines`) or sits in a fixed-height box. Only decorative glyphs in fixed circles (menu, gear, back, the "2P" badge) keep their size (`allowFontScaling={false}`, hidden from screen readers). `apps/mobile/test/textScaling.ts` checks these rules on Home, New game, the game screen, its cards (opening, pass, mother question, choose a double and its pass), the game menu and a resign dialog, the game-end sheet and match end, in English and Greek.
- **Contrast:** `packages/ui/src/themes.test.ts` checks every text and background pair the app uses against WCAG AA (4.5:1). `textDisabled` was raised to `#A89274` (it was 3.1:1), so locked labels like "Coming soon" stay readable.

## Board flip

The theme numbers points from the view of the player at the bottom (the **viewer**). The engine numbers points in White's order. `boardModel.ts` maps each absolute point to the viewer's own number (`absoluteToOwn`). So the viewer always moves 24 → 1, with home at the bottom left. Turning the board to the other player is only a change of viewer: a left–right mirror, as the wireframes ask.

## Home board side (#175)

**Settings → Board → Your home** (`homeBoardSide`) says where your home is on a real board, seen from your seat. The phone shows that board on its side (portrait), so on screen your home is always at the bottom.

| Setting | Real board | On the phone | Your checkers go |
|---|---|---|---|
| `right` (default, as before #175) | home bottom right | start bottom right, home bottom left | counterclockwise |
| `left` | home bottom left | start bottom left, home bottom right | clockwise |

How the mirror works:

- `boardLayout(theme, mode, side)` (`BoardCanvas.tsx`; `portraitLayout(theme, side)` for the tall board) gives the theme's layout, or with `left` the same layout mirrored left to right (`mirrorLayout` in `packages/themes`). Every point rect, point base, bar half, tray, field and the dice area moves to the other side. Point numbers and point tones stay with their points. One mirrored layout is kept per theme layout, so its identity is stable.
- Everything else reads the layout, so it all follows: the triangles, stacks, pinned checkers, the "to enter" stacks, trays, highlights, ghost targets, tap and drag hit tests (`interaction.ts`) and the flights (`flights.ts`). `boardModel.ts` does not know about the mirror.
- `InteractiveBoard` (game, tutorial) and `StaticBoard` (rules pictures, board picker, dev board) read the setting through `useHomeBoardSide()`. `StaticBoard` takes a `homeSide` prop for the side picker's pictures. A change applies at once, also in a running game.
- The tall board art is **not** mirrored: the image layers, the board shadow and the lamp stay as they are, so the light keeps coming from the same side. This works because every bundled portrait layout is left–right symmetric (`isMirrorSymmetric`; `packages/themes/src/themes.test.ts` checks each theme). A new theme must be symmetric too. The classic (landscape) art is not symmetric, so its image layers are mirrored (`artMirrored`, see [Board layouts](#board-layouts-184)).
- The flip (pass-and-play) still works on top of it: it changes the viewer, the mirror changes the layout.
