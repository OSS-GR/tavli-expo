## Rules pages (#112, #124)

- `/rules/portes`, `/rules/plakoto`, `/rules/fevga` and `/rules/asodio` each show a full page. `/rules/tavli` explains the match rotation and links to its three games (`TAVLI_GAMES`; Asodio is not in the rotation). Any other id says there are no rules.
- Opened from Home (**How to play**), the in-game menu (**How to play Plakoto**: the game on the board, in Tavli the game of the rotation) and New game (the **i** button on each game card). Only the route files call `router`.
- `features/rules/content.ts` lists each page's sections in reading order: goal, start position, dice and turns, how checkers move, hitting / pinning / no hitting, the special rules (Plakoto mother, Fevga first checker and six-in-a-row bans, Asodio entering and the loading lock, quarter by quarter, the draw), bearing off and points. Shared sections (`rules.common.*`) appear on every page; a game can add its own line to a shared section (for example Plakoto: no bearing off while pinned).
- The texts live in one i18n group, `rules.*`. Each body is a few short lines split by `\n`; each line shows as one bullet paragraph. Greek uses tavli words: πόρτα, πλάκωμα, μάνα, εξάπορτο, μάζεμα, μονό / διπλό, βαθμοί.
- The texts describe the **default presets** only (`DEFAULT_PRESET_BY_GAME`): Plakoto `motherRule: "offerConcede"` (resign for diplo or play on; both mothers pinned is a draw), Fevga runner rule to pass the opponent's mother and the prime bans P1, P2 (points 6–11) and P3, Asodio `asodio.kafeneio` (A1–A6: a 1-2 or a double opens the board, a double enters four, entered checkers wait until all 15 are in, the quarter lock, pins, no bearing off while pinned, a deadlock is a draw). When a default flag changes, update the page.
- The start position is a small `StaticBoard` (60% of the width, at most 240 pt, the theme's aspect ratio) built from the engine's start layouts and the preset's direction, with White (light checkers) at the bottom. It is one image for screen readers ("The board at the start of Fevga"). The Asodio picture is an empty board with both "to enter" stacks (15 each).
- Every section title is a header. Nothing has a fixed height, so the page works at large text sizes, and it scrolls.
- Each game's page starts with **Learn to play on the board**, which opens that game's lessons (#128, #149, #159). The Tavli page has none.

## Tutorial (#128)

A one-minute lesson in Portes on the real board, before the first game.

- **Where it opens.** After the welcome flow, Home shows a **Learn to play** card (`features/home/LearnCard.tsx`) under the Continue card: **Start the lesson** and **Not now**. The card shows until the tutorial is finished or dismissed. It always opens again from Settings → Help → **Learn to play** and from the top of the Portes rules page (**Learn to play on the board**). The `from` param says where it was opened (for `tutorial_started`).
- **Settings → Help (#159).** **Learn to play ›** opens a sheet (`features/tutorial/TutorialSettings.tsx`, in the shared `Sheet`) with one button per game: Portes, Plakoto, Fevga, Asodio (`TUTORIAL_GAMES` in `features/match/routes.ts`, names from `games.*`), and **Cancel**. A pick closes the sheet and pushes `tutorialHref('settings', game)`. The sheet scrolls, so it fits a small phone with a large font.
- **Lessons** (`features/tutorial/lessons.ts`), each on a fixed position with fixed dice, in the `portes` preset, White (you) at the bottom:
  1. `move`: one checker, one die (13 → 9 with the 4).
  2. `bothDice`: one checker with both dice (5 and 3).
  3. `double`: 3-3 moves four times.
  4. `makePoint`: two checkers to point 7 make a door, which blocks the opponent.
  5. `hit`: hit Black's lone checker on 9.
  6. `enter`: enter from the bar (the 5 lands on a door; the 3 enters).
  7. `bearOff`: bear off the last two checkers.
- **Plakoto, Fevga (#149) and Asodio (#159) lessons.** Four short lessons each, on what is new after Portes. They open from the top of each game's rules page (**Learn to play on the board**, `?game=plakoto`, `?game=fevga` or `?game=asodio`; no `game` param = Portes) and from Settings → Help. Each lesson plays in its game's default preset (`lessonRules`: `DEFAULT_PRESET_BY_GAME`, so Asodio is `asodio.kafeneio`), so the rules are those of a real game.
  - Plakoto: `pin` (pin Black's lone checker on 9; no bar), `pinned` (your checker on 16 is pinned and cannot move; move another), `mother` (pin Black's mother on your point 1), `plakotoBearOff` (bear off, none pinned). After the mother pin, the "resign or play on" card of the app default (`offerConcede`) shows over the board (`MotherLessonCard`, the game's `GameCard` with the same two buttons). It is the opponent's card, so a tap only says what that answer does. The card scrolls when it is taller than the board (small phone, large font), and screen readers skip the board under it.
  - Fevga: `sameWay` (the start in opposite corners; run the first checker with both dice), `holdPoint` (one dark checker holds point 6, so the 4 is blocked), `runner` (13 → 11 passes Black's start, then a checker leaves 24), `sixInARow` (a 5 from 24 would fill points 19 to 24, so only the 6 is played; `fevga.md` edge case 12).
  - Asodio (owner version, `asodio.md`): `aceDeuce` (all 15 wait off the board; the 1-2 opens it and enters on 24 and 23), `enterAll` (3-3 enters the last four on 22; the checkers on the board wait while one is still off, A1 and A2), `quarterLock` (14 checkers on 14 to 18 are frozen behind the last one on 20; move it up, then any checker moves), `pinLast` (pin Black's last checker alone on your 10; Black's whole army is frozen, A6). The board marks frozen checkers as in a game (`asodioMarks`).
  - The end card offers **Play Plakoto** / **Play Fevga** / **Play Asodio** (`/new-game?mode=bot&first=plakoto`: that game vs the Easy bot).
- **No RNG, no second rule book.** `startLesson` builds a `GameFlow` from the lesson's board and rolls the lesson's dice (`roll` with `dice`). The board offers the engine's `legalNextMoves`, filtered to the moves the lesson teaches (`lessonMoves`). So the board lights up only the checker to touch (`InteractiveBoard` `cue`), and after a tap only the right target. Taps and drags work as in a game.
- **Each lesson**: a title, one line of text, the lit checker; a wrong touch (a tap on another checker, a miss after picking the checker, a drop on a wrong place) shakes the text and shows a hint, with the `invalidMove` haptic. After success: a success line and **Next**. **Skip** is always in the top bar. "Lesson 2 of 7" shows the progress.
- **End card**: "You know the basics!", **Play your first game** (replaces the tutorial with `/new-game?mode=bot&first=1`: Portes vs the Easy bot with the standard opening roll (#207), whatever was chosen last; `firstGameSetup` in `setup.ts`) and **Home**.
- **State** (`features/tutorial/progress.ts`): `new`, `dismissed` (Not now, or Skip) or `done`, per lesson set, saved as `{ version, status }` in the `Storage` port: Portes under the key `tutorial` (as before #149), the others under `tutorial.plakoto`, `tutorial.fevga` and `tutorial.asodio` (#159). A new game only adds a key, so the keys saved before it still load. The store keeps Portes in `status` (the Home card offers only Portes) and the others in `variants`. It never leaves the phone, so it needs no consent. It is apart from Settings, so a settings reset does not bring the card back. `done` never goes back to `dismissed`.
- **Analytics** (`tutorialAnalytics.ts`): `tutorial_started`, `tutorial_step`, `tutorial_finished`, `tutorial_skipped`, each with the lesson set (`game`), through the `Analytics` port, so only after **Allow usage data**. An analytics error is logged and dropped. See [Analytics events](analytics-events.md).
- **Accessibility.** Titles are headers. The board's label says what the lesson asks ("Your checker on point 13 is lit. … Move it 4 points, to point 9."). The hint and the success line are a live region and are announced. With **Reduce motion** on there is no shake and no checker flight; the hint still shows.
- No swipe back on the tutorial route (`gestureEnabled: false`), like the board: it would fight the drag. Skip goes back where it came from (Home after a deep link).

## Local matches

- `features/match/setup.ts`: the new-game choices (`MatchSetup`): mode, game (default Portes, see [Choosing the game](#choosing-the-game-109)), match length 1 / 3 / 5 / 7 (default 3), bot level, names, the opening roll house rule (`openingRoll`, see [House rules](#house-rules-207)) and the rules version of Plakoto and Asodio (`rulesVersions`, see [Rules version](#rules-version-241)). `sanitizeSetup` turns any saved value into a valid setup: a game that is locked in this mode becomes Portes, a bot level the game has no bot for becomes the easiest one it has.
- `features/match/sessions.ts`: a session is the setup plus a game store (`createGameStore`, with `rules: gameRuleSet(setup.game, setup.openingRoll, setup.rulesVersions)`). MVP1 keeps **one** match at a time; `start` replaces the one before. Ids are `local-<time>-<random>`.
- Vs bot the human plays **White** (bottom), the bot **Black**. The board never flips.
- Names come from the setup, in the app language (`features/match/seats.ts`): "You" / "Bot · Easy", or the typed names with "Player 1" / "Player 2" as the default. The engine only stores labels (`enginePlayers`). Texts about the human use `_you` strings ("You win the game!").

## Choosing the game (#109)

- The game picker on New game lists the games that are open as radio cards: **Portes**, **Plakoto**, **Fevga**, **Tavli** (all three in rotation) and **Asodio**. Default: Portes, or the last game chosen in this mode. Locked games are dashed chips; a tap shows the toast "Coming soon: Asodio".
- Each game uses its engine preset (`gamePreset`, `gameRuleSet` in `setup.ts`):
  - Portes, Plakoto, Fevga, Asodio: a **one-game match**. Plakoto and Asodio use their chosen rules version (#241, default `plakoto` and the owner version `asodio.kafeneio`). The engine's Plakoto and Fevga presets keep the shared rotation Portes → Plakoto → Fevga, so the app sets `match.rotation` to the one game ("single game" in `tavli-match.md`).
  - Tavli: the `tavli` preset, Portes → Plakoto → Fevga → Portes → … Each game uses its own default preset and the match flags of `tavli`; the engine scores every game the same way (mono 1, diplo 2) per `docs/rules/tavli-match.md`. With another Plakoto version (#247) the match preset is that version with the Tavli rotation: the engine plays it in the Plakoto games only (`gameRules`).
- **When a game is locked** (`isGameOpen`): when the engine or the board cannot play every game of its rotation (`isGameBuilt` checks the engine registry and `BOARD_GAMES` in `features/match/setup.ts`; since #124 every game is built), and, vs bot, when the bot has no level for it. Pass-and-play needs no bot, so every built game is open there.
- **Bot levels per game** (`features/bot/levels.ts`, `botLevelsByGame`): for each variant the app asks the bot `supportedLevels(variant)` (bots from `createBot` have it), or else plays a probe turn from that variant's start at each level and drops the levels that throw `NOT_IMPLEMENTED`. Tavli gets the levels that work in all three games, because one match has one bot level. `services.ts` keeps the result in `botLevels` (a `GameLevels` record). A new bot variant or level shows up by itself when the bot package gets it.
- Each game card has an **i** button that opens its rules page (`/rules/<game>`) without choosing the game. With a rules version that is not the default (#241) the link adds `?version=<preset>`, and the page starts with what that version changes.
- The level control shows the levels of the chosen game. Switching to a game without the chosen level shows that level locked and plays the easiest level; switching back brings the choice back.
- The menu names the game being played: "Game 2 · Plakoto" (`currentGameLabel` in `summary.ts`, `menu-game-label`). The classic board layout (#184) also shows it at the top of the screen; the tall layout has no room for it. It stays on the finished game until **Next game**. The menu's **How to play** opens the rules of the game on the board, in its rules version (#241).
- The game-end sheet names the next game: "Next: game 3 · Fevga" (`nextGameLabel`). The match-end rows name each game: "Game 2 · Plakoto".

## House rules (#207)

A first part of Kafeneio mode (#58). The full rules editor is not in the app yet.

- New game has a **House rules** part in both modes with one choice, **Opening roll** (a segmented control): **Standard** = `useOpeningDice` (default, the federation rule: the starter plays the two opening dice), **Roll again** = `rerollBoth` (the starter rolls both dice again, so a double is possible), **Choose** = `chosenStarterRolls` (the players pick who starts; vs the bot the human picks). The hint under it says what the chosen rule does. It replaced the "Who starts?" info box of wireframe 4.
- It is the engine flag `match.openingRoll` (`docs/rules/tavli-match.md`). `gameRuleSet(game, openingRoll)` puts it into the rules of the match, so it applies to a one-game match and to every game of a Tavli match (after a draw, a new opening uses the same rule). A rematch keeps the rules (and starts with the last winner, no opening).
- The last choice is remembered per mode, like the other options (`lastSetup.ts`). A saved setup with no or an unknown rule gets the default.
- The game screen only sends engine actions: `rollOpening` (both other rules) or `chooseStarter` (the starter card, `starter-card`: one button per player, "Maria starts" / "I start", the person first vs the bot). With `rerollBoth` the opening result says "Maria starts (6-3) and rolls again."; Roll waits until it closes. See `docs/architecture/board-screen.md`.
- The save slot holds it in `setup.openingRoll`; the log's rules hold it too, and a save where they differ is `broken`. Saves before #207 load with the federation rule. Each history record keeps `openingRoll` (from the match rules; records before #207, or with an unknown rule, read as `useOpeningDice`: the record is never dropped for it). `game_started` sends it as a value id (`docs/architecture/analytics-events.md`).
- Online games (`apps/server`) do not offer `chosenStarterRolls`; nothing online uses this choice yet.

## Rules version (#241)

The second part of Kafeneio mode (#58): start from another engine preset.

- In **House rules**, under Opening roll, Plakoto and Asodio get a **Rules version** choice («Παραλλαγή»), a segmented control of the game's presets (`RULES_VERSIONS` in `setup.ts`, the default first):
  - **Plakoto:** **Standard** = `plakoto` (default, the owner rule `offerConcede`) and **Federation** = `plakoto.federation` (ΕΟΠΤ 3.3.iii: a pinned mother wins 2 once the pinner's point 24 is empty; both mothers pinned is a draw).
  - **Asodio:** **Standard** = `asodio.kafeneio` (default, the owner version), **Greek** = `asodio.aceyDeuceyGreek` and **American** = `asodio.aceyDeuceyAmerican` (`docs/rules/asodio.md`, "Presets").
  - Portes and Fevga have one preset each: no choice shows.
  - **Tavli** (#247) shows the Plakoto choice for its Plakoto game: **Rules version (Plakoto)** («Παραλλαγή (Πλακωτό)»), Standard (default) / Federation. Portes and Fevga keep their one preset; Asodio is not in the rotation. It is the same `rulesVersions.plakoto` as a Plakoto match, so a choice made for one shows for the other.
  - The hint under it says what the chosen version changes (`newGame.rulesVersion.<key>Hint`; `VERSION_KEYS` maps a preset id to its key, because a dot nests i18next keys).
- Screen readers (#243): both house rule groups have a "Standard" option, so each radio's name starts with its group: "Opening roll: Standard", "Rules version: Federation", in Tavli "Rules version (Plakoto): Federation" (`newGame.houseRules.optionLabel`). `SegmentedControl` takes `accessibilityLabelFor(option)` for this; the visible label stays short. The other New game groups (game, length, bot level, board view) share no option name.
- `MatchSetup.rulesVersions` holds one version per game (`{ plakoto, asodio }`), so the choice of one game stays when the player picks another. Only the version of `setup.game` is played (`versionedGameOf`: Plakoto's for a Tavli match): `gamePreset(game, versions)` gives the match preset, and `gameRuleSet(game, openingRoll, versions)` resolves it with the opening roll and the rotation of the game (`gameRotation`). A Tavli match is `tavli` with the standard Plakoto, else the Plakoto version (`plakoto.federation`) with the Tavli rotation. `versionsOfPreset(game, preset)` reads the versions back from a match preset. `sanitizeSetup` gives a missing or wrong version the default (saves and last choices before #241 have none). The first game after the tutorial (`firstGameSetup`) plays the default version of its game and keeps the last choice of the other game.
- The save slot checks that the log's preset is `gamePreset(setup.game, setup.rulesVersions)`; else the save is `broken`. Saves before #241 load with the default presets, which their logs hold. A Tavli save from before #247 holds `tavli` whatever its setup's Plakoto version: it loads with the standard Plakoto.
- Each history record keeps `preset` (the match preset, `match.rules.presetId`; `tavli` or the Plakoto version for a Tavli match, #247). A record before #241, or with a preset that is not one of its game's, reads as the game's default. History shows a version that is not the default after the length (and after the opening roll): "· Rules version: Federation" («Παραλλαγή: Ομοσπονδίας»), from `history.rulesVersion` and New game's label; a Tavli match says "· Rules version (Plakoto): Federation" («Παραλλαγή (Πλακωτό): Ομοσπονδίας», `history.rulesVersionPlakoto`). `recordSetup` keeps it.
- `game_started` sends `rulesVersion`: the preset of the game in play (`flow.game.state.rules.presetId`), a value id (`docs/architecture/analytics-events.md`). In a Tavli match with Federation: `portes`, `plakoto.federation`, `fevga`.
- The rules page (`/rules/[variant]?version=<preset>`, `parseRulesVersion` and `versionSection` in `features/rules/content.ts`) starts with a section "This game: federation rules" (`rules.versions.<key>`) that says what the version changes. The rest of the page still describes the default rules, except the Points section, which follows the version's scoring (#245, below). The default version adds no param and no section. The menu's **How to play** in the Plakoto game of a Tavli match opens `/rules/plakoto?version=plakoto.federation` (the game state's preset). The Tavli page (`/rules/tavli`) takes no version: `rulesHref` adds one only for a version of the page's own game.
- The engine and the bot already play every preset. The hint depth and budget are per preset (`features/hint/hint.ts`), and the review uses the game's own rules. Acey-Deucey's chosen double after a 1-2 shows the "choose a double" card (#205); the screen only sends the engine's `answer`.
- **Scoring.** Acey-Deucey American scores 1 point per checker the loser has not borne off (`scoring.method: perChecker`). `winType` (`features/game/turnNotices.ts`) then gives its own win type, `perChecker`, for every win (#245): the screens say "+1 point" or "+2 points", never "mono" or "diplo", and the game-end chip stays outlined (`isStrongWin`: filled only for a diplo or a Tavli backgammon). Every caller switches on the win type alone, so TypeScript makes each one handle `perChecker`. `isDiplo` (history) counts only a 15-point win (no checker off) as a diplo. The analytics `result` of `game_finished` (`resultKind`) uses `isDiplo` too, so a 2-point American win is `mono`.
- **Texts that name points follow `rules.scoring.method` (#245).** The points always come from the engine result (`result.points`); the app adds no rule. With `perChecker`:
  - New game's match length hint says "First to 5 points. 1 point per checker left." («1 βαθμός για κάθε πούλι που μένει», `newGame.length.hintPerChecker`). `gameScoring(game, versions)` (`setup.ts`) reads the method of the chosen preset; every other version and game keeps "Mono = 1, diplo = 2." (`NewGameScreen.lengthHint.test.tsx`, English and Greek).
  - The resign dialog says "Bot wins 5 points, because you have 5 checkers left to bear off: 1 point each." (`game.resign.perChecker`, `perCheckerYou`).
  - The game-end sheet's chip is "+5 points" (`game.end.points`, plural), and its reason line is "Nikos did not bear off 5 checkers: 1 point each." after a bear-off, or "You resigned and did not bear off 5 checkers: 1 point each." after a resign (`game.end.checkersLeft`, `resignedPerChecker`).
  - The rules page's Points section says 1 point per checker left, and that a resign counts the same way (`rulesPage(game, version)` in `features/rules/content.ts`, `rules.common.scoring.bodyPerChecker`).

## Save and resume (#36)

- One save slot (`savedMatch` key) in the `Storage` port (MMKV). `features/match/savedMatch.ts` writes `{ v, flowSchema, id, setup, savedAt, log }` as JSON.
- **Load = replay.** `parseSavedMatch` checks the versions (`SAVE_VERSION`, the engine's `MATCH_FLOW_SCHEMA_VERSION`) and rebuilds the flow with the engine's `replay(log)`, which checks every event again. The log holds every die, so no RNG is needed. Anything wrong (bad JSON, another version, an edited log, setup and log that differ) gives `broken`: the save is removed and never throws.
- `features/match/sessions.ts` saves when a match starts and each time the flow comes to rest (`isRestPoint`: awaiting a roll, the opening roll, or game over). So a confirmed turn, a pass, a game end, the next game and a rematch all save. Moves in the middle of a turn wait for Confirm.
- `saveNow()` saves at once, mid-turn too: when the app leaves the foreground (`useSaveOnBackground` in `app/_layout.tsx`) and on **Exit to home**.
- A finished match is removed from the slot. A rematch is saved as a new match (a new log, the same id).
- The opening roll house rule (#207) is `setup.openingRoll` and must match the log's rules (`match.openingRoll`); a save without it loads with the federation rule.
- The rules version (#241) is `setup.rulesVersions`: the log's preset must be the one of `setup.game`. A save without it loads with the default presets. A Tavli save (#247) keeps the Plakoto version of its Plakoto game; one from before #247 loads with the standard Plakoto, which its log holds.
- The game (#109) is `setup.game`. The save shape did not change, so `SAVE_VERSION` stays 1: older saves always held `game: 'portes'`, and a save with no game loads as Portes. `parseSavedMatch` checks that the game is built and that the log's rules are that game's preset and rotation; else the save is `broken`. A saved game the bot cannot play any more keeps its game (the log already holds it) and plays the easiest level.
- A save that fails (a storage error) only logs a warning. The move stays played, and the next save tries again.
- After an app restart, `get(id)` loads the save into a new game store (`createGameStore({ flow })`); new dice come from a new local RNG. A saved bot level this build cannot play becomes Easy.
- **Home** reads the slot each time it shows (`useFocusEffect`). With a save it shows **Continue game**: game, opponent, score, target and time since the save ("12 min ago"). A broken save shows the toast "Your saved game could not be opened." once.
- **New game** with a save: Start asks "Start a new game? Your saved game will end." (Start new / Cancel).

## History (#142)

The player's past local matches and simple stats, offline. Finished online matches come last, in their own **Online** section, from the server (#313, see [Online matches in History](#online-matches-in-history-313)); they never go into the records or the stats.

- **Records** (`features/history/records.ts`). `sessions.ts` adds one record through its `history` dep (`appSessions.ts` wires `historyStore`):
  - when a match is over (`end: 'finished'`): won to the target, a resigned game that reaches it, a game lost on time that reaches it (`timedOutBy`, #170), or **Resign match** (`resignedBy`). Once per match: a rematch in the same session is a new record. The id is `<session id>@<time>`.
  - when a new match replaces one that had started (`end: 'abandoned'`, no winner), in memory or only in the save slot after a restart. "Started" (`hasStarted`): a game finished, or someone rolled for a turn or moved. The opening roll alone does not count. A match left before that gets no record.
  - when `get(id)` loads a saved match while another one is in memory (rare: that one's save failed). The one in memory is left: `abandoned` and `game_abandoned`.
- **Stale saves** (#150): a match is named by its session id and its `matchNumber` (1, then +1 per rematch). The save slot and every record carry it. A save of a match the history already holds (`hasMatchRecord`) is stale: the match finished but the app died before the slot was cleared, or it was left but the slot could not be removed. No time is compared, so a phone clock set back cannot make a live rematch save look stale. `peek()` and `get(id)` treat a stale save as no save (not on Home, not loaded, not recorded again) but never delete it; `start()` and `end(id)` do. Saves and records from before #150 have no number (`null`) and are never stale.
- A record holds: `id`, `endedAt`, `mode`, `game`, `botLevel` (vs bot; `null` in pass-and-play), `names` (pass-and-play names as typed), `targetPoints` (any positive whole number loads, so old records stay; the row shows it as it is, and only the setup used for labels falls back to the default length), the final `score`, `end`, `winner`, `resignedBy`, `timedOutBy` (the loser, when the last game ended on time and that ended the match; `timedOutBy` in `features/match/summary.ts`; `null` in records from before #170), the number of `games`, `diploWins` per player (games won with 2 points or more, mars), drawn games (`draws`), `matchNumber`, `openingRoll` (the house rule, #207; `useOpeningDice` in records from before #207) and `preset` (the rules version, #241; the game's default preset in records from before #241). With Tavli scoring "2 or more" is right: a single is 1 point and every double is 2 (no checker off, a pinned mother, a resign or timeout in such a position). Only `backgammonScores3` gives 3, and that is a double too. Acey-Deucey American (#241) scores 1 per checker left (`perChecker`): there only 15 points (no checker off) is a diplo (`isDiplo` reads the match's `scoring.method`). A test checks the scoring of every game and version.
- **Storage**: `{ v: 1, records }` JSON under the key `history` in the `Storage` port, newest first, at most 200 (`MAX_HISTORY_RECORDS`). Loading keeps the stored order (no sort by time, so a clock set back does not reorder the list). It never leaves the phone, so it needs no consent. The save slot of the match in play (`savedMatch`) did not change. Bad JSON or another version reads as an empty history; a bad record is dropped (also a time that makes no valid date), and of two records with one id the newest stays. A storage error, or a history error inside `dispatch`, only logs.
- **Screen** (`/history`, header shown, in the app group of `_layout.tsx`). With no record: "No matches yet" and **Play** (New game vs bot). Else **Stats**, then **Matches**, newest first. Each row is one element for screen readers: "Portes · vs Bot (Easy), You 3 – 1 Bot · first to 3, Won, Oct 27, 2026". After Resign match the score part says who resigned: "You 0 – 2 Bot · first to 3 · you resigned" (#181). A match with an opening roll house rule (#207) shows it after the length: `history.openingRoll` wraps New game's short label, "You 3 – 1 Bot · first to 3 · Opening roll: Roll again" («Πρώτη ζαριά: Ξαναρίχνει») or "· Opening roll: Choose" («Πρώτη ζαριά: Διαλέγουμε»). The screen reader label reads it too, because it is built from the score line (#221). The federation rule (`useOpeningDice`, also every record from before #207) shows nothing extra. A rules version that is not the default follows (#241): "· Rules version: Federation" («Παραλλαγή: Ομοσπονδίας»), "· Rules version: Greek" («Παραλλαγή: Ελληνική»). `recordSetup` (`format.ts`) keeps the record's `openingRoll`, so the setup is correct; an unknown rule gets `useOpeningDice` and shows nothing. The badge is **Won** / **Lost** vs the bot, "Maria won" in pass-and-play, **Not finished** for a left match. A match lost on time says so: "Won on time", "Lost on time", "Maria won on time" (#170). The date uses the phone locale when it speaks the app language (`dateLocale`), else the app language.
- **Stats** (`features/history/stats.ts`), per game (Portes, Plakoto, Fevga, Tavli, Asodio; only games with a match) and per bot level: played (finished and left), won, lost, win rate (`won / (won + lost)`, whole percent of finished matches, "—" before one is won or lost), diplo (mars) wins and draws. A left match is neither won nor lost ("Not finished"), so it does not change the win rate. A drawn game counts on its own, not as a loss. Pass-and-play has no "you", so its line has only played and draws.
- **Entry points**: a small **History** link on Home under **How to play**, and Settings → History: **Match history ›** and **Clear history**, which asks first (Clear / Cancel). Clear is off while the history is empty.
- No numbers in fixed boxes and no line limits: `textScaling.test.tsx` checks the screen. The local rows and the online rows share one card (`MatchList.tsx`).
- **Known limits.** A match the player leaves and never replaces (it stays in the save slot) gets no record. A record that fails to save (a storage error) stays in memory and goes into the next save, but is lost if the app closes first; there is no retry.

## The bot turn

- The app reaches the bot only through the `BotRunner` port. `services.ts` wires `InlineBotRunner` and lists the levels the bot can play per game (`botLevels`, from `features/bot/levels.ts`, see [Choosing the game](#choosing-the-game-109)). Today Easy and Medium work in Portes, Plakoto, Fevga and Asodio (the Asodio heuristic, #125), and Hard works in all four (a trained net per game, #147, #156 and #164). So Tavli, which needs a level in all three of its games, has Hard too. No level shows locked with the real bot; the locked ("coming soon") state stays for a bot that lacks a level.
- `features/bot/botTurn.ts` plays one bot turn: wait, **roll** (the flow's `Rng` rolls), ask the runner and wait `thinkingDelayMs` at the same time (the short "forced" range when there is only one legal play; right after its own roll, never before the dice have landed), play the moves **one by one**, with a short extra pause after a hit, then **confirm** once the last checker has landed. Settings → Bot speed (Slow, Normal, Fast) picks the timings; see [Bot turn timing](board-screen.md#bot-turn-timing-172). It sends the same actions as a human, so each move flies on the board.
- `GameScreen` starts the bot turn when nothing covers the board (opening result, pass card, end cards) and the screen is not paused. The menu and screens on top (Settings) pause it; `cancel()` clears the timers and cancels the runner. On resume the bot keeps moves that start its plan, or takes them back.
- During the bot turn the board takes no touches and the controls row is a locked box: "Bot's turn…", then "Bot rolled 4-2 and is moving…". The bot's dice lie on the board (#184).
- Opening roll vs bot: one tap rolls both dice. A bot that cannot move shows the pass card for 3.5 s, or until you tap OK ("Bot cannot move. Your turn." in the wireframe). Your own pass card waits for OK; the bot starts its turn after that (#178).

## Online games (#254)

The app logic for one online game. It lives in `features/online/`. The screen (#255) is in [Board screen → Online mode](board-screen.md#online-mode-255). The server is the truth: see "Move flow" and "Turn timers" in [Online game service](online-game-service.md).

- **`createOnlineStore({ transport, gameId, me, now })`** (`onlineStore.ts`) is a Zustand store over the `GameTransport` port. `me` is the player id of this phone; the store finds your side in `view.players`. The calls carry no player id: the transport knows the caller.
- **Views.** The store subscribes at once. Each view with a newer `version` replaces the local flow: `{ schemaVersion, match: view.match, game: view.game, log: [] }` (`viewFlow`). A repeat or an older version is ignored, so your local moves stay. `flow` and `view` are `null` until the first view arrives. A load error (for example `NOT_FOUND`) sets `loadError`.
- **Your turn on the phone.** `dispatch` takes the engine's `MatchAction`s. `move` and `undo` go through `applyMatchAction` on the local flow, as in `gameStore.ts`: the engine is the only judge of a move, and Undo stays on the phone. The store follows the view (`waitingFor`, `prompt`, `game.state.toMove`), never "the players alternate": an Asodio turn can give you another roll.
- **Calls.** `confirm` sends every move of the turn in order (`sendTurn` with the view's `version` as `expectedVersion`). A `move` with `autoConfirm` sends the turn as soon as it is complete (the engine never confirms online). `answer` (the Plakoto mother question, the Asodio chosen double) goes through `sendAnswer`, and `resignMatch` through `resign`. The engine checks a confirm and an answer first. Online there is no game resign: `resign` is refused.
- **Sending.** From a call until the server has it **and** its new view is here, `sending` is `true` and every action is refused. A call that fails always clears it.
- **Refused with no call** (each one adds 1 to `refusals`, so the same refusal twice is still a change): the steps the server does by itself (`roll`, `rollOpening`, `nextGame`, `chooseStarter`, `timeout`), an action for the other side, a move, undo, confirm or answer when it is not your turn, any action before the first view, after the match or after `dispose()`. `resignMatch` works on either turn.
- **Errors** set `notice` (the screen shows it, then calls `clearNotice`), add 1 to `noticeCount` (it names each notice, so a screen reader hears a repeated one again) and set `lastError`:
  - `STALE_VERSION` → `stale`: the local moves are dropped; the store shows the newest view.
  - `TIMED_OUT` → `timedOut` ("Your time ran out"): the local moves are dropped. The server saved the time out, so its view follows.
  - Any other server code → `refused`: the local moves are dropped.
  - A network error (anything that is not `GameTransportErrorData`) → `sendFailed`: the local moves stay, so the player can confirm again.
- **Time left** (`timeLeft.ts`, pure): `timeLeft(view, localNow, offset)` is `turnDeadline - (localNow + offset)`, never below 0, `null` when the match is over. The deadline is on the server clock. The store keeps `clockOffset` (server time - phone time): 0 for the first view after you open a game (it may be old), then the highest `estimateOffset` of the newer versions: `turnDeadline - TURN_TIME_MS[pace]` is the server time of that save. A view always arrives after its save, so an estimate is never above the true offset, and a late view (after a reconnect) cannot pull it down.
- **`dispose()`** stops the subscription. The store then ignores every later view and call result.
- **Sound and haptics** (#271): the store plays none itself. The screen reads them from the flow: your moves from its log, the rest from each new view (`onlineFeedback.ts`, see [Board screen → Online mode](board-screen.md#online-mode-255)). The view of your own turn plays only the server's next roll, so your moves never sound twice.
- **Not like `GameStoreState`.** The store has `flow`, `matchKey` (`o1`, `o2`, …), `lastError` and `dispatch` like the local store, so `GameScreen` can read it with few changes. But `flow` is `null` until the first view, there is no `rematch` (an online match has no rematch yet), and `dispatch(confirm)` returning `true` only means the call started: the result comes later (`sending`, `notice`, the next view). No analytics events are sent yet.
- **Tests** (`onlineStore.test.ts`, `timeLeft.test.ts`) run the real server model: `createMemoryGameServer` from `@tavli/online` with two stores, one per player. The server runs no timers: a test moves its `now` and calls `runDueTimeouts()`. A wrapper transport rejects with a plain `Error` for a network error.

## Your online games (#297)

Home lists the active online games of the saved session, live. Code: `features/online/myGames.ts` (the hook and the pure parts), `features/online/OnlineGameList.tsx` (the rows and states), `features/home/HomeOnlineGames.tsx` (the Home card) and `features/online/MyGamesScreen.tsx` (the whole list).

- **When it shows.** Only in a build with an online server (`auth` and `games` from `@/services`) and a saved session. The app never makes a guest for it. Better Auth's `ready()` asks the server for the session, so Home calls it only when this phone has signed in before: `accountHint.ts` keeps a flag in `storage` (`onlineAccount`). `signInForOnline`, `useOnlineAccount` and `useOnlineConnection` set it when they see a user; only a sign-out (`onChange` gives `null`) clears it. Better Auth's `ready()` never rejects: it gives `null` when it cannot reach the server too, so a `null` from `ready()` shows "did not load" with **Try again** and keeps the flag (a launch with no network must not hide the list for good). A phone that never played online makes no network call on Home. `app/index.tsx` reads the flag again each time Home comes into view.
- **Live.** `useMyOnlineGames(auth, games)` follows `auth.onChange` and `auth.ready()`, and watches `games.watchMyGames({ status: 'active' })`. On Convex that is the `listMyGames` query through `onUpdate`, so the list follows every save; the in-memory server sends a new list after each save and each new game (see [Online game service](online-game-service.md)). A sign-out hides the list; a new user loads the list from scratch.
- **Rows** (`gameRowText`): "Portes vs friend", "Your turn" or "Waiting for your friend", your points first ("3–1") and the time left ("23 h left", from `timeLeft` and `clockText`). "Your turn" rows come first (`yourTurnFirst`); each part keeps the server order, newest change first. The clocks tick while the screen is in view (`usePhoneClock`, as often as the fastest game needs: `listTickMs`), and at once when the screen comes into view or the app comes back to the front (`AppState`), since timers run late after the background. The clock offset is a lower bound (`clockOffset`): the newest `updatedAt` was saved before the list left the server, so a phone that runs behind is set right. A tap opens `game/<gameId>`.
- **Limit.** Home shows `HOME_GAME_ROWS` (3) rows. With more, **See all (N)** opens `app/online-games.tsx` (`ONLINE_GAMES_HREF`), which lists them all. That route is in the guarded app group and goes Home in a build with no online server.
- **States.** Loading ("Loading your online games…"); no game (Home shows no card; the whole list says "No online games right now"); did not load, with **Try again**. A watch that fails after a list came keeps that list and adds a note ("This list may be out of date") with **Try again**, also when that list was empty. Convex reconnects by itself, so a short loss of the network changes nothing.
- **Screen readers and large fonts.** Each row is one button; its label reads the game, the turn, the score ("3 to 1") and the time. The card title is a header. Home and the whole list scroll; no text has a line limit.
- **Analytics.** None. Never send a game id or the user id.
- **Finished games** are in History (#313, see [Online matches in History](#online-matches-in-history-313)), through the same hook with `finished`.
- **Tests.** `myGames.test.ts` (order, clock offset, ticks, row text in English and Greek), `HomeOnlineGames.test.tsx` (a session that does not load: "did not load", the flag kept, no guest, then **Try again** and a sign-out; loading; no game; order and **See all**; a row reads as one item and a tap opens it; a friend's move, a new game and a resign change the list; the clock counts down, and is set right when the app comes back to the front; a failed load and **Try again**; a lost connection keeps the list, and the note shows over an empty list; sign-out; Greek), `navigation.test.tsx` (no flag: Home never reads the session; after an invite Home lists the game and a tap opens it; **See all** → the whole list → a game; the whole list with no session: "did not load", no guest) and `textScaling.test.tsx` (Home, the whole list and a failed load, in English and Greek). The Jest fake's `games.watchMyGames` acts as the user signed in when the watch starts; `test/afterEnv.ts` clears the flag before each test.

## Online matches in History (#313)

History shows the local matches from the phone. Below them, an **Online** section lists the finished online matches of the saved session, live from the server. Code: `features/history/OnlineHistory.tsx` (the section), `onlineHistoryRow` in `features/history/format.ts` (the row text) and `useMyOnlineGames` (`features/online/myGames.ts`, the hook of [Your online games](#your-online-games-297)).

- **When it shows.** The same rule as Home: a build with an online server (`auth` and `games` from `@/services`) and the account note (`hasOnlineAccount()`), read once when History opens. With no note, History reads no session and makes no call. It never makes a guest. A sign-out hides the section. A finished list that is empty shows no section.
- **Empty state.** "No matches yet" and **Play** show only when the phone has no record and the online section has no match: `OnlineHistory` calls `onMatches(true)` while it loads or lists a match (`HistoryScreen`'s `online` is a render function that gets `onMatches`). The screen keeps one tree, so the section never mounts again when the empty state goes.
- **Live.** `useMyOnlineGames(auth, games, 'finished')` watches `games.watchMyGames({ status: 'finished' })`: newest change first, at most `MAX_LISTED_GAMES` (50). A match that ends while History is open shows up.
- **Rows.** The title and score of Home's rows (`gameTitle`, `gameScore` in `features/online/gameText.ts`, pure): "Portes vs friend", your points first and the length ("3–1 · first to 5"), then the winner as a badge ("You won" / "Your friend won", «Κέρδισες» / «Κέρδισε ο φίλος σου») and the day the match ended (`updatedAt`, `formatDate`). A screen reader reads one item per row: "Portes vs friend, 3 to 1, first to 5, You won, Oct 28, 2026". A row has no tap: Home opens only games in play. A resigned match keeps its score (often 0–0); the list does not say who resigned.
- **States.** The list states of Home (`OnlineGameList` with `renderRows`): loading, did not load with **Try again**, and the "may be out of date" note after a lost connection.
- **Not in the stats.** Online matches never go into `historyStore`, the records or the stats: the server keeps them.
- **Analytics.** None.
- **Tests.** `OnlineHistory.test.tsx` (no note: no section and no call; no server; finished matches newest first with the winner and no tap; the local stats and records unchanged; no local record but an online match: no empty state, one watch; no note: the empty state; the empty state again after a sign-out; no finished match; a failed load and **Try again**; a lost connection; a session that does not load; sign-out; Greek), `history.test.ts` (the row text: your points first, no winner, Greek), `navigation.test.tsx` (no note: History never reads the session; History from Home lists a finished match and adds no record) and `textScaling.test.tsx` (the section and a failed load, in English and Greek).

## Turn notifications (#285)

The app tells a player "Your turn" in an async online game. The server sends the push ([Online game service → Push](online-game-service.md#push), #280); the app asks for the permission, registers the push token and opens the game on a tap. Code: `apps/mobile/src/features/notifications/`.

- **Ports.** `PushNotifications` (`packages/contracts/src/push-notifications.ts`: permission, prompt, token, taps) on `expo-notifications` (`@tavli/notifications-expo`, its `README.md`), and `PushTokens` (`register`, `remove`) on Convex (`createConvexPushTokens`, `@tavli/backend-convex`). `services.ts` exports them as `notifications` and `pushTokens`. Both are `undefined` with no Convex URL, and both are made on first use (`onlineServices.ts`), so the app makes no call at start. Contract checks: `pushNotificationsContract` and `pushTokensContract` in `@tavli/contracts/testing`.
- **When the app asks** (`turnPushes.ts`, `createTurnPushes`). Never at first launch, and never for a live game. After **Make an invite** with the async pace (`app/friend/index.tsx`), or after Accept on an async invite from Convex (`app/invite/[code].tsx`; a dev invite never asks), the route calls `turnPushes()?.offer()`. The offer (`TurnPushPrompt.tsx`, a `Sheet` in the root layout) shows unless the player turned them on already, said **Not now**, or the phone blocks them: "Turn notifications?" and "We tell you when it is your turn, so you do not have to keep the app open". It shows also when the phone allows pushes already (Android 12 and older allow them from the install, with no prompt): a token never goes to the server without the in-app choice. **Turn on** is that choice (`turnedOn`, saved). It shows the system prompt only while the phone has not been asked; on a phone that allows pushes it shows no prompt and sends no analytics event. A prompt that fails on the phone (the native call throws; the adapter gives `undetermined`) is no answer: nothing is saved, no event goes out, and the app may offer again. **Not now**, a tap on the scrim, or a no to the system prompt is saved (`turnPushes` in storage, `{ declined, turnedOn }`): the app does not offer again by itself.
- **Settings → Notifications** (`TurnPushSettings.tsx`) shows the state: on (only when the player turned them on in the app and the phone allows them), off (with **Turn on**, which works also after Not now and on a phone that allows pushes but was never turned on in the app) or off in the phone settings (with **Open phone settings**, `Linking.openSettings`). It reads the permission again when the app comes back to the front, then calls `sync()`: a player who allowed them in the phone settings gets the token registered. A build with no online server has no Notifications section.
- **Register.** `sync()` needs the in-app choice (`turnedOn`), a phone that has used an online account (`hasOnlineAccount()`, the `hasAccount` dep; without one it does not even call `auth.ready()`), a granted permission and a signed-in user (`auth.ready()`; it never makes an account). It registers the token once per app run for that user, and again when the token changes (`onTokenChange`) or another user signs in; on a token change it removes the old token (best effort). Once registered in a run, it does not ask Expo for the token again until the token changes. It runs after **Turn on**, after an offer when the player turned them on before, when Settings comes back to the front, and when an online game route finds the saved session (`features/online/connection.ts`). The calls run one at a time, in order, and never reject.
- **Sign-out.** Sign out only through `signOutOfOnline(auth, turnPushes())` (`features/online/account.ts`): it calls `unregister(() => auth.signOut())`: the token is removed (`removePushToken`) while the session still works, and the sign-out runs in the same queue, so a `sync()` asked for meanwhile runs after it and cannot put the token back. A phone that never turned them on in the app (and registered nothing in this run) asks Expo for no token and calls no remove. A no to a later system prompt keeps an earlier **Turn on**, so the sign-out still removes a token the server may hold. The sign-out also clears the saved choice (`turnedOn` and `declined`): it belongs to the user who made it, so the next person who signs in on the phone gets the offer and needs their own **Turn on**. Then `forgetOnlineAccount()` (the Home note, [Your online games](#your-online-games-297)). There is no sign-out button yet.
- **A tap** (`pushTaps.ts`, `usePushTaps` in `app/_layout.tsx`) opens `game/<gameId>` from the push's `data.gameId` (a "your turn" and a "game over" push, #316, carry the same `data`, so both open the game): the tap that opened the app (`lastTap`) and later taps (`onTap`). Each tap opens once. An id that is not an online game id (a local save slot id, or not an id at all) opens nothing. A tap while the welcome flow is due waits, like an invite link (`usePendingInvite`): the welcome group's `redirectTo` is then the game. A push that comes while the app is open is not shown (no foreground handler).
- **Analytics.** `push_permission_answered` with `answer` (`granted` or `denied`) only, through `quietly()` (`pushAnalytics.ts`), and only when the system prompt was really shown. Never the token or the user id.
- **Android.** The adapter makes the channel `default` (named "Your turn" in the app language) before the first prompt: Android 13 shows no prompt before an app has a channel. The server sends no channel id, so its pushes use it. The `expo-notifications` config plugin in `app.json` sets the color and the default channel. The owner steps (EAS project id, APNs key, FCM v1 key, the icon) are in `packages/adapters/notifications-expo/README.md`.
- **Screen readers and large fonts.** The offer moves focus to its title (`useFocusOnShow`) and scrolls. It turns with the phone on the game route (`turns`). It sits in the root layout, so each screen it can cover hides itself: `useTurnPushOffering()` (`appTurnPushes.ts`) is true while it shows. The invite wait screen (`InviteWaitScreen`, prop `covered`) hides its content from screen readers (`importantForAccessibility="no-hide-descendants"`, `accessibilityElementsHidden`, `collapsable={false}`), and its cards take focus (`useFocusOnShow(active)`) and are read only after the offer closes. The online game route passes `covered` to `OnlineMatchScreen`, which pauses and covers the board as under its own cards. A new screen the offer can cover must do the same.
- **Tests.** `turnPushes.test.ts` (the offer, also on a phone that allows pushes, Not now kept, Turn on, a failed prompt, the token once and again on a change or a new user, the old token removed, no server call without an online account, unregister, a sync during sign-out, a failed call), `turnPushFlow.test.tsx` (the real root layout with `renderRouter`: an async invite offers and a live one does not, a phone that allows pushes sends no token until Turn on, the wait screen hidden from screen readers under the offer, Accept on an async invite, never at first launch, an online game route sends no token without the in-app choice, a tap opens the game, the opening tap, bad ids, a tap during the welcome flow, Settings, a phone with no online play), `onlineServices.test.ts` (made on first use, the shared client). In Jest `@/services` has `createMemoryPushNotifications` and `createMemoryPushTokens` (`test/onlineFake.ts`, reset before each test); drive a tap with `onlineFake.notifications.tap(…)` inside an async `act`.

## Differences from the wireframes

- The menu is a `Modal` sheet inside the board route, not a `menu.tsx` form-sheet route. So the board folder has no `menu.tsx`, and Settings from the menu is a push, not a modal.
- The celebration is a small Reanimated effect, not Lottie (see [Board screen](board-screen.md)).
- Home has no board art yet (art task); the art area shows the tagline.
- The "Play vs bot" subtitle lists the bot levels: "Easy, Medium or Hard" (Greek: "Εύκολο, Μέτριο ή Δύσκολο"). `levelList` in `HomeScreen.tsx` builds it from the levels the bot plays in at least one game (`anyGameLevels` in `features/bot/levels.ts`), so it changes when the bot gets a level (#182).
- A broken save on the board route shows the "could not be opened" card (not a dialog), with a Home button.
- The game also saves mid-turn when the app goes to the background or the user exits (the wireframe accepts losing those moves; we keep them).
- New game has a small **i** button on each game card that opens its rules page (not in wireframe 4).
