# ADR-0006: Bot strategy and licensing

- **Status:** Accepted (owner accepts by merging the PR that adds it)
- **Date:** 2026-10-26
- **Issue:** #9

## Context

Players need a bot for all four variants, offline, at easy/medium/hard levels. Research: [`docs/architecture/bot-ai.md`](../architecture/bot-ai.md).

## Options

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| Ship GNU Backgammon | Very strong | **GPL-3.0** — conflicts with App Store terms in a closed app; Portes only |
| Use wildbg (MIT/Apache, CC0 nets) | Strong, legal to ship | Standard backgammon only; WASM on Hermes not proven |
| **Write our own in TypeScript** | Works for all variants; reuses our engine; no license risk | We must build and tune it |

## Decision

1. **Own bot in pure TypeScript** in `packages/bot`: a *search* plus a per-variant *evaluator*.
2. **Easy / medium (MVP1):** heuristic evaluator, 1-ply search, controlled random noise for weaker play.
3. **Hard (v1.0):** small neural net per variant trained by **TD(λ) self-play** in Node with our own engine. 1-ply plus 2-ply on the top 3–5 moves. Runs on the phone in plain TypeScript, off the UI thread (`react-native-worklets`).
4. **Never ship GPL code or weights.** gnubg and wildbg may be used only as local benchmarks.
5. The app talks to bots only through the `BotRunner` port (inline in MVP1, worklet in v1.0, server later if needed).

## Consequences

- One bot design covers all four games.
- The hard level needs real training work in v1.0 (tracked in #52).
- Asodio needs final rules (open question Q5) before its bot.
